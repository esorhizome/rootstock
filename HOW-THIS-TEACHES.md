# How this repo teaches

This is the teaching contract. Every lesson in *rootstock* follows it, and anyone adding pages should follow it too. It exists because the intended readers have been failed by teaching materials before — usually in the same handful of ways — and those failures are avoidable by design.

## Who the pages assume you are

No diagnosis is required at the door. The pages assume that attention wanders, that memory drops things, that handwriting may cost more than it gives, and that confidence arrives late if at all. These are treated as design constraints, the way an architect treats gravity — not as deficits to be pitied, and never as reasons to water anything down. The material stays real; the *packaging* does the accommodating.

## The working agreements

**1. One idea per page.** Each lesson opens by naming its single idea in one sentence. If a draft contains two ideas, it becomes two pages.

**2. Forgetting is planned for.** Lessons open with a short **Previously** box that re-grounds whatever the page depends on, in two or three lines. Symbols are re-introduced every time they appear — a page that uses *V* says "V, the push" again, even if the page before did too. No page ever says "as you'll recall."

**3. Every formula travels with a plain-words twin.** `V = I × R` never appears without something like *"push equals flow times squeeze."* The twin is not a dumbed-down version; it is the meaning, wearing comfortable clothes.

**4. Nothing requires handwriting.** Every *Try it* offers a path through typing, clicking, speaking aloud, or a calculator. Handwriting is welcome for those it serves and never assumed. Your handwriting is not your maths.

**5. Calculators are always allowed.** Arithmetic-by-hand is a separate skill from understanding, and this repo teaches understanding. Professionals reach for calculators without shame; so do we.

**6. Encouragement is small, specific, and factual.** *"If the coin example made sense, you have the whole idea"* is the house style. *"Amazing job!! You're crushing it!!"* is banned — it reads as either noise or pressure, and the readers here deserve neither. Light, and often; never syrup.

**7. Difficulty is stated honestly.** When a page is fiddly, it says so — *"most people need two passes at this one"* — because pretending something is effortless quietly blames the reader when it isn't.

**8. Every page has an exit ramp.** Lessons end with *"A fine place to stop"* and mean it. There are no cliffhangers, streaks, or guilt mechanics anywhere in this repo.

**9. Understanding delayed, never denied.** Borrowed from the house-rules of *FloralFight*: losing a thread costs you time, not access. Nothing locks. A page that defeated you in March will hold still until you feel like a rematch.

## Words this repo does not use

**"Just," "simply," "obviously," "clearly," "easy," "of course," "trivial."** Each one is a tiny accusation aimed at whoever needed a second pass. Deleting them costs nothing and removes the sting.

**Vocabulary from the royalty family** — that entire word-family, every synonym and cousin — is also retired here, as a house style. When a page wants to gesture at beauty or stature, it reaches for other shelves: *graceful, luminous, poised, refined, gleaming*. Elegance survives the substitution entirely.

## About the pedestal

A recurring, checkable fact, placed here on purpose: **working engineers and mathematicians look things up constantly.** They keep reference sheets. They re-derive formulas they've used for decades. They type `resistor value for LED` into search bars. The difference between them and a beginner is not a gift and not a memory bank — it is time spent in good company with the ideas, plus the learned calm of *knowing that looking things up is the job*.

There is no gifted crowd on a hill. There are only people at different points along the same slow, lookup-riddled path. This repo is the path, with benches.

## How a visit can be shaped

**Five minutes:** one lesson's *One idea* line, its Previously box, and its first section. Stop there. That was a real visit; it counts.

**Twenty minutes:** one full lesson, including the *Try it*. Maybe the first section of the next.

**Returning after a long gap:** read one Previously box. Done. (This is the whole ritual, and it is enough — see [START-HERE.md](START-HERE.md).)

## For anyone writing new pages

Use [08-templates/lesson-template.md](08-templates/lesson-template.md). Keep lessons under roughly 800 words. Say what the page is for, keep one idea, add the twin to every formula, offer a no-handwriting *Try it*, close with the exit ramp, and date the bottom. If a sentence sounds like a cheer squad or like a gatekeeper, it isn't house voice yet.
