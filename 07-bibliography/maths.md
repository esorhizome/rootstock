## Playgrounds — start here on low-energy days

### Mathigon
<https://mathigon.org/>
**What:** A self-described mathematical playground — narrative interactive courses plus Polypad, a table of drag-and-drop mathematical objects.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely (funded by school licensing, not paywalls)
**Account:** optional (progress-saving only)
**Fit:** The gold standard for this repo's readers: touch-first, luminous, story-shaped, zero handwriting.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### PhET Simulations — maths collection
<https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/filter?subjects=math>
**What:** University of Colorado's research-backed drag-and-explore simulations: fractions, graphing, algebra, trig.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely
**Account:** optional (not needed to run sims)
**Fit:** Pure point-and-drag with instant visual feedback — arguably the most dysgraphia-friendly maths resource anywhere.
**Caveat:** Sims explore rather than explain; pair with a lessons resource.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### Desmos Graphing Calculator
<https://www.desmos.com/calculator>
**What:** Browser graphing calculator with sliders, tables, animations.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely
**Account:** optional (saving graphs only)
**Fit:** Typed input, immediate pictures; the companion tool for [branch 01's grid page](../01-maths/03-the-grid-under-everything.md).
**Caveat:** A tool, not a curriculum.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### GeoGebra
<https://www.geogebra.org/>
**What:** Free graphing/geometry/algebra tool suite plus a large library of ready-made interactive activities.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely
**Account:** optional (saving work only)
**Fit:** Very hands-on; drag things, watch maths respond.
**Caveat:** Many modes — can feel cluttered on first open; arrive via a specific activity link when possible.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

## Courses and lessons

### Khan Academy
<https://www.khanacademy.org/>
**What:** The vast non-profit: short videos plus instantly-marked practice, arithmetic through calculus.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely (optional paid AI tutor exists; core needs none of it)
**Account:** optional (progress/mastery tracking)
**Fit:** Short chunks, typed/clicked exercises, hints on demand, no shame mechanics.
**Caveat:** Its sheer size can overwhelm; enter via one course, not the front page.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### Mathorio
<https://www.mathorio.com/en>
**What:** Newer interactive lesson platform — 334+ stepwise lessons, arithmetic through trigonometry, six languages.
**Cost:** ✓ free ("no subscriptions, no paywalls. Ever")
**Account:** optional (XP/streak gamification — ignorable)
**Fit:** Short interactive steps with instant feedback.
**Caveat:** Small young team; drafts are AI-generated then teacher-reviewed (their own disclosure).
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### Math Is Fun
<https://www.mathsisfun.com/>
**What:** Plain-language explanations, illustrated dictionary, small quizzes and games; long-running.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely
**Account:** none
**Fit:** Very short per-topic pages, low jargon — a kind first-explanation site.
**Caveat:** Dated look; depth caps around early-secondary level.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### Mathplanet
<https://www.mathplanet.com/>
**What:** Swedish non-profit's free US-curriculum courses (pre-algebra → algebra 2) with practice tests.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely
**Account:** none
**Fit:** Clean short text lessons; not interactive.
**Caveat:** US school-course framing.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### ChiliMath
<https://www.chilimath.com/>
**What:** Former teacher's free tutorials, basic maths through algebra/trig, with worked examples.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely
**Account:** none
**Fit:** Readable in short bursts; text-only.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### Math Antics
<https://mathantics.com/> (videos also free on YouTube)
**What:** Short, friendly animated video lessons, arithmetic through basic algebra.
**Cost:** ◐ videos free; printable worksheet packs ~$20/yr (skippable here — worksheets are handwriting anyway)
**Account:** none to watch
**Fit:** ~10-minute videos, warm conversational tone, no writing.
**Caveat:** Styled for school-age viewers; the maths is solid regardless.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

## Intuition and beauty

### BetterExplained
<https://betterexplained.com/>
**What:** Analogy-first essays hunting the "aha" behind concepts (including a free calculus course).
**Cost:** ◐ articles free; an optional ebook is sold
**Account:** none
**Fit:** Written directly against the pedestal myth — ideal for confidence repair; essay-length, so budget attention accordingly.
**Caveat:** New content slowed since ~2022; existing essays age well.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### 3Blue1Brown
<https://www.3blue1brown.com/> · <https://www.youtube.com/@3blue1brown>
**What:** Grant Sanderson's animated essays on linear algebra, calculus, and more — the most beautiful maths animation being made.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely
**Account:** none
**Fit:** Visual intuition at its peak; videos run 10–25 minutes and assume some prior exposure — a *second* touch on a topic, rarely the first.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### Seeing Theory ⌛
<https://seeing-theory.brown.edu/>
**What:** Brown University's interactive visual introduction to probability and statistics.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely
**Account:** none
**Fit:** Low text, high interaction; graphics carry the meaning.
**Caveat:** ⌛ Archived (unmaintained but fully working, 2026-08); scope is probability/stats only.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### Immersive Linear Algebra
<https://immersivemath.com/ila/index.html>
**What:** A linear-algebra textbook whose figures are all draggable, embedded live in the text.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely
**Account:** none
**Fit:** Interactive figures in prose — but undergraduate-level; shelve until vectors feel friendly.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### NRICH
<https://nrich.maths.org/>
**What:** University of Cambridge's library of rich "have a go" problem activities.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely
**Account:** none
**Fit:** Gentle, playful problem framing; discrete small activities.
**Caveat:** Organized by UK school ages — adults dig a little for their level.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

## Reference shelves — for looking things up

### Mathematics LibreTexts
<https://math.libretexts.org/>
**What:** Huge open-access library of college maths texts.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely
**Account:** none to read
**Fit:** A reference forest, not a garden path — visit with a specific question.
**Caveat:** Tone/quality varies book to book (it's an aggregation).
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### Paul's Online Math Notes
<https://tutorial.math.lamar.edu/>
**What:** Lamar University instructor's famously clear notes, algebra through differential equations, with fully worked solutions and free PDFs.
**Cost:** ✓ free entirely
**Account:** none
**Fit:** Plain, patient text; the classic "my course lost me, explain it again" shelf.
**Caveat:** Starts at algebra; calculus-dense later.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### OpenStax — Maths
<https://openstax.org/subjects/math>
**What:** Peer-reviewed, openly-licensed full college textbooks (Prealgebra → Calculus, Statistics).
**Cost:** ✓ free online + free PDF (print copies cost)
**Account:** none to read
**Fit:** Formal textbooks — rigorous backup, not a gentle on-ramp.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

## Practice drills — flagged honestly

### Transum ◐
<https://www.transum.org/>
**What:** Hundreds of short interactive puzzles/starters, arithmetic to A-level.
**Cost:** ◐ student activities free; £69/yr tier is for teachers (answer keys, ad-removal)
**Account:** none found for the free activities
**Fit:** Bite-sized clicked/typed puzzles.
**Caveat:** Ads on the free tier; automated checks were partially blocked — spot-check on first visit.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*

### IXL ◐⚠
<https://www.ixl.com/math>
**What:** Adaptive practice questions, pre-K through calculus, instant feedback.
**Cost:** ◐ ten free questions per day; real use is subscription ($9.95+/mo)
**Account:** ⚠ effectively required, and the free tier is a taste, not a path
**Fit:** The questions themselves are well-made and typed-answer.
**Caveat:** Daily wall plus upsell prompts — a rough combination for self-discouragement-prone readers; listed because the drills are good, flagged because the wrapping isn't.
*Last checked: 2026-08-19*
