# Numbers you can trust

**One idea:** Fractions, decimals, percents, and negatives are not four subjects — they are four outfits on the same creature, and you may greet the creature in whichever outfit feels friendliest.

> **Previously** — [How to read a maths page](01-how-to-read-a-maths-page.md) made one promise: slow reading is correct reading. It applies here. A calculator is welcome throughout; that is house policy, not a concession.

## One creature, four outfits

Take the amount "three quarters."

As a **fraction** it wears `3/4`. As a **decimal** it wears `0.75`. As a **percent** it wears `75%`. On a **picture** it is three slices of a pizza cut into four. Same amount, four outfits. Nothing new happens to the quantity when it changes clothes — which means every skill you have in one outfit secretly works in the others.

Most people trust one outfit more than the rest. Percents, usually — battery icons and volume sliders have given everyone years of quiet percent practice. That trusted outfit is a legitimate home base. When a fraction looks hostile, you are allowed to walk it over to percent-land and deal with it there.

## The fraction secret

A fraction is a division that hasn't happened yet. That is the entire secret.

`3/4` *means* `3 ÷ 4`. Type `3 ÷ 4` into any calculator and it answers `0.75` — the fraction taking off its coat. This works on every fraction, including unfriendly-looking ones: `7/8` is `7 ÷ 8` is `0.875`. If two fractions need comparing, let the calculator undress both and compare the decimals. That is not cheating; that is knowing what the notation means, which is the actual skill.

(Why do fractions exist at all, if they're divisions in waiting? Because sometimes the waiting is the point — `1/3` stays perfectly exact, while `0.3333…` has to trail off. Fractions are how numbers stay poised instead of trailing off.)

## Negatives are directions, not punishments

A negative number is an ordinary number pointed the other way. Money: `+50` is having fifty, `−50` is owing fifty. Temperature: `−4°` is four steps below the zero line, same size step as `+4°` above it. Every negative number lives on the same line as the positives — left of zero instead of right, equal citizen, opposite direction.

Two things follow, both gentle. Subtracting is *stepping leftward* — and if you step leftward past zero, you land in the negatives, no drama involved: `3 − 5 = −2`. And "minus a minus" is *turning around twice*, which faces you forward again — which is the whole mystery of why `5 − (−2) = 7`, dissolved.

Game screens will later add one twist — their vertical numbers often grow *downward* — and [the grid page](03-the-grid-under-everything.md) handles it when you get there.

**Try it (optional, ~2 minutes, nothing to hand-write):** Type `5 ÷ 8` into any calculator — phone calculators count. Look at the answer, then say aloud: *"five eighths, zero point six two five, sixty-two and a half percent — same creature."* You've now personally run the machinery this page is about.
