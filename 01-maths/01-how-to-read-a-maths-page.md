# How to read a maths page

**One idea:** Maths is read slowly by everyone — including professionals — and the slowness is the method, not a malfunction.

> **Previously** — This page stands alone. It depends on nothing.

## The misread

Here is what tends to happen. You open a page with symbols on it. Your eyes move at normal reading speed, the way they would over a novel. Halfway through the second line, meaning falls away. And a familiar verdict arrives: *I'm not a maths person.*

The verdict is built on a false assumption: that maths is read like prose. It is not — by anyone. A working physicist can spend ten minutes on four lines and call it a productive morning. Mathematicians re-read a single sentence five times, go make tea, and re-read it twice more. That is not remedial behaviour at the top of the field; that *is* the field.

So the experience of slowing down, re-reading, and feeling the meaning arrive late — the exact experience that felt like proof you don't belong — is what doing maths feels like from the inside, for everyone. You were doing it correctly and grading yourself against a reading style that doesn't exist.

## Symbols are nicknames

A formula is a sentence wearing compression. `V = I × R` is the sentence *"the electrical push equals the flow times the squeeze"* — three words shortened to three letters because engineers write them constantly, not because letters are more serious than words.

Nicknames are convenient for people who already know the friend. They are cold to people meeting the friend for the first time. So in this repo, every formula travels with its plain-words twin, every time it appears. You are always allowed to read only the twin. The twin *is* the meaning; the symbols are its handwriting.

## When a page stops making sense

That moment — meaning drops, fog rolls in — is information about the *page*, not about your mind. It means the page moved faster than it taught. There are two honest moves, and both count as studying:

**Re-read once, slower,** saying the plain-words twin aloud if there is one. Speaking recruits a second channel and often carries meaning across gaps that silent reading leaves open.

**Or leave.** Close the page. A page that defeated you today will hold still — pages are patient in a way people rarely get to be. Returning tomorrow with a rested mind is a well-documented study technique, not an escape.

What is *not* on the list: pushing through fog at full speed while the verdict machine warms up. Nothing useful lies down that road.

## What "getting it" feels like

Smaller than expected. Not fireworks — more like a small click, or the fog thinning around one sentence while the rest stays murky. One click per visit is a normal, respectable yield. They accumulate. That's the entire mechanism.

**Try it (optional, ~1 minute, nothing to hand-write):** Read this aloud, once, at half speed: *"Push equals flow times squeeze."* That sentence is Ohm's law, the first equation in the electronics branch, and you now hold its meaning. The symbols can wait for you as long as needed.
